## Preserved semantics

- **Readiness is the v0.7.21 algorithm, byte for byte.** `internal/readiness`
  is unchanged and the cross-release differential (below) is byte-identical
  over 3,000 generated graphs. `Compute` does not mutate its inputs
  (`TestComputeByteForByteInputImmutability`).
- **Local approval, dependency readiness and integrated proof stay separate;**
  a v0.7.21 approval whose hash moved fails closed (`approval_content_drift`,
  `local_approved: false`, `ready: false`) and recovers only through unlock
  and a human-approved re-lock (carry-over, below).
- **`rests_on` is still the only claim-to-claim edge.** A brief's `rests_on`
  is a one-hop baseline from the brief to a claim; no claim rests on a brief,
  nothing traverses from a brief, and no brief byte enters `ContentHash`,
  `LockedClaimHash`, a claim cause or a claim finding. The claim-side packages
  do not import `internal/briefs`:
  `go list -deps ./internal/lock ./internal/readiness ./internal/catalog
  ./internal/model ./internal/lint ./internal/manifest ./internal/loader
  ./internal/reaudit | grep -c internal/briefs` prints `0`. The one lint that
  reads a brief, `source-internal-drift`, does so through the
  `lint.BriefContentHash` hook that `internal/briefs` sets; `internal/lint`
  itself imports no briefs package.
- **Clearing one boundary never clears another.** A brief's `reaudit
  --confirm` clears only the brief; the claims resting on the moved claim
  stay `review_pending` through their own `direct_dependency_change`
  (carry-over, step 6 below). Re-lock after unlock keeps a brief's baselines
  (`TestARelockKeepsAReviewPendingBriefPending`,
  `TestUnlockEditLockKeepsAReviewPendingBriefPending`).
- **Reads write nothing.** Every read-only candidate command left the
  v0.7.21-written store byte-identical, and every refused command left a
  too-new store byte-identical.
- **New viewer consumers read, never recompute.** Home reads
  `readiness.Assessment.ReviewCauses`, the brief pages read
  `briefs.Evaluation`, BRIEFS rows read the registered lint's verdict.

## Approved changes and where they are documented

All in `CHANGELOG.md` under `[0.7.22]`:

- `migrated_from` retired; `LockedClaimHash` loses its line, so every
  locked claim's approval hash moves once and re-locks on the human's yes
  (Upgrading; Removed). Decision: Nitin, NIT-191 (2026-09-29).
- Tracks retired; `claims[].tracks` leaves the catalog and
  `nodes[].tracks` / `groups.tracks` leave the graph payload (schema stays
  3; both keys were `omitempty`). Decision: Nitin, NIT-180/NIT-184.
- The lock store gains a `briefs` map at version 4, earned only by the first
  brief record; a store newer than the binary is refused (`store_too_new`).
- A claim's `internal` source on a brief pins the brief's content hash, not
  the file (NIT-198 F1).
- New viewer projections: Home, brief and feature pages, Briefs index,
  BRIEFS rows. Every rendered viewer grows (wc -c table in the CHANGELOG).

Known oracle exceptions: none. The v0.7.21 engine and the candidate agree
exactly on every generated case.

## Worst-case bounds and budgets

Claim readiness: unchanged from v0.7.21 because the algorithm is
unchanged. One query O(V + E) plus a BFS per cyclic SCC entry, no path
enumeration; all claims R ≤ V·F records, each with one witness of length ≤ V,
so catalog bytes are O(V·(V+E_b)·D) on deep or dense graphs. Budgets
asserted in tests: `TestAuditScale` (fact, compute and output-byte bounds),
`TestConformanceEndToEndGraphScaleBounds` (2 s non-race wall time, 512 MiB
TotalAlloc, `conformance.MaxOutputBytes` per file), and catalog.json below
64 MB.

Briefs (N briefs, R longest `rests_on`, C claims, S internal sources, B brief
bytes): every pass is linear in briefs, baselines, rows or bytes up to a
sort, as each per-ticket note tabulates; nothing enumerates a path or a pair.
`Evaluate` O(C + N·R) hashes; BRIEFS rows ≤ N·R + S; Home's output is
independent of corpus size. Every serialized brief byte is charged to the
viewer's 64 MiB bound (`check`) or to a 64 MiB briefs budget (`serve`
without a conformance report). Declared acceptance budget for the brief
projections: at the default caps (60 briefs, 12 per folder, 2,000 words,
3 images) each benchmark below completes in under 100 ms and 64 MB
allocated with output under the 64 MiB bound; raised-cap rows are adversarial
evidence that growth stays linear.

## Adversarial results

- Deep chains (32/64/128), layered dense DAGs (8/16/24×5), mixed-cause dense
  graphs and 3,000 random cyclic and acyclic graphs terminate with
  identical facts, witnesses and catalog bytes to v0.7.21; every witness is a
  real edge path; every catalog is under 64 MB.
- Brief shapes: a brief on many claims and many briefs on one claim (every
  baseline drifted), a gone claim (`brief-rests-on-missing`), 2,000 briefs in
  one duplicate group (message bytes bounded per finding), a 70 MiB
  two-word brief (refused at the payload, measured in the NIT-204 note),
  2,000 features at 50 `rests_on` each. Growth follows briefs, baselines,
  rows and bytes, never paths.
- Store shapes: a v0.7.21 store with retired snapshot keys, a version-4
  store rewritten by v0.7.21, and a version-5 store. None can make an
  approval look valid; the newer store is refused before any write.

## Gaps and unresolved risks

- **Memory before a refusal at raised caps.** The 64 MiB bound refuses the
  output, not the memory spent building it: at a human-raised 2,000 features
  the feature view allocates about 686 MB, and a 2,000-brief review payload
  about 910 MB, before the render is refused. At the default caps neither
  exceeds 24 MB. Charging those views to the budget as they are built is
  recorded backlog in the NIT-201 and NIT-203 notes; it is not a readiness or
  approval defect.
- `serve` without a conformance report renders claims unbounded, as it has
  since before this range (NIT-204 note); briefs there have a budget of
  their own.
- Timings come from one developer laptop under normal load.
- **The merge-commit rebind** required by `docs/RELEASING.md` must be redone
  on the commit actually tagged: re-run `xver.sh`, `carry.py`,
  `brief-state.sh`, `projections.sh`, `named.sh`, the two scale tests and the
  whole-tree gates there.
